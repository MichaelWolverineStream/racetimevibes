Bands

The bands are set by hand in the plan file, one set of ranges per day group, and are not ranked from turnout. Each range gives Eastern Time start hours, both ends included; the hours of neither range are normal. Each cell is a start hour. Between categories

A between category shares few racers with the main ones, so its races start only in a gap between two main races: 1 or 2 hours after one and 1 or 2 hours before the next, one race to a gap. They come on top of a set’s races a day. Two races less than 2 hours apart lose their pair score times the smaller race's expected entrants, charged to the smaller race (the later one on a tie) and capped at that race's own expected entrants. A missing score counts as 1. A lower score costs fewer entrants.

Holidays

Thu 2026-11-26, Fri 2026-11-27, Thu 2026-12-24, Fri 2026-12-25, Thu 2026-12-31, Fri 2027-01-01 count as Prime days.

The planner treats these as holidays: New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Black Friday, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, New Year's Eve. A holiday takes the Prime day group, with its bands and its races a day. Featured races keep their weekday and hour.

Method and limits

Every slot the alttpr.racing schedule ever held is joined to the race it became. A slot that never became an archived race was cancelled (rooms with fewer than two entrants are never archived), so it counts as zero entrants. Over the last 365 days, a race’s expected turnout is its category’s average in that four-hour part of the Eastern Time day, adjusted for the exact hour of the date’s day group and for the mode.

Days fall into three groups: Work (Monday to Wednesday), Casual (Thursday and Friday) and Prime (Saturday, Sunday and the common US days off: New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Black Friday, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, New Year's Eve). The plan file sets each group’s prime time and quiet hours by hand; the other hours are normal. Work: prime 12:00 to 15:00 (4 hours); quiet 01:00 to 07:00 (7 hours); the other 13 hours normal. Casual: prime 12:00 to 17:00 (6 hours); quiet 02:00 to 07:00 (6 hours); the other 12 hours normal. Prime: prime 11:00 to 18:00 (8 hours); quiet 02:00 to 04:00 (3 hours); the other 13 hours normal.

The plan file lists the categories and modes; the planner picks none of its own. Between categories race only in a gap between two main races, on top of the main races. Each set runs a fixed number of main races a day in the Work, Casual and Prime groups: Light 7, 8, 9 (55 a week); Medium 8, 9, 10 (62 a week); Full 9, 10, 11 (69 a week); Bonanza 10, 11, 12 (76 a week).

Each category first gets a featured race in prime time, on a weekday of its own, at the same hour every week, picked from its own record at that hour. Each date then takes its main races at least 2 hours apart, at the hours that hold the most prime races, then the most normal ones. Outside prime time 3 hours apart is preferred, so a race goes to a quiet hour before a normal-hour gap shrinks to 2 hours. A short gap is two consecutive main races less than 3 hours apart, unless both start in prime time. A lookahead keeps a date from ending so late that the next date cannot reach its races a day.

Main prime-time races are given out first, then normal, then quiet. Each race goes to the main category furthest below its share of that band, and a band is shared by expected turnout. A race avoids the category that ran at the same hour a week earlier, then the one that ran a day earlier, and never breaks a daily or weekly limit or an event’s rule. A between category races whenever it is behind its own share of a band, in a gap 1 or 2 hours after one main race and 1 or 2 hours before the next, one race to a gap.

A category never races in a band where its rooms fired less than 10% of the time. Its prime-time races run its favourite mode, and its other races take its modes in turn.

Limits. Expected turnout is an average of the past year, not a forecast of growth or decline. Cancelled slots are inferred from missing archived races. The bands are set by hand, not learned: the page shows the model’s value of each hour next to them, so a band that no longer fits the demand can be seen. The demand model cannot see that a category’s races draw fewer racers the more of them run on one date, so the strongest categories take the most races. A band can be closed to a category by bad luck when it has few slots there. Holiday factors come almost entirely from weekends. Overlap scores are an upper bound on conflict between categories, and the ladder has rarely run races an hour apart. Spoiler’s overlap scores are weak evidence and high, so its between races score low even though the plan file treats it as a category that shares few racers. A mode with no recent slots counts at its category’s average.
